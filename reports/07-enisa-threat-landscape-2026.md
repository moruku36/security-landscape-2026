# ENISA Threat Landscape 2026

## Positioning

ENISA provides a public-sector and EU-centric lens, useful for validating vendor findings against a different observation model and for understanding critical-infrastructure, geopolitical, NIS2, resilience, and availability concerns.

## Interpretation

ENISA adds three dimensions that vendor IR reports can underweight:

1. **Availability** — DDoS and service continuity.
2. **Geopolitics** — hacktivism and state-aligned pressure.
3. **Critical dependencies** — essential entities and digital ecosystem interconnection.

## Architecture actions

- Treat availability and DDoS resilience as first-class security outcomes.
- Map supplier/service dependencies for critical services.
- Design incident and recovery processes around business-service continuity.
- Align architecture ownership with NIS2-style essential/important service criticality where relevant.
- Include geopolitical scenarios in threat modeling for critical/public services.

## NIST/CIS relevance

This report's resilience emphasis maps naturally to:

- NIST CSF 2.0 GOVERN / IDENTIFY / RESPOND / RECOVER.
- CIS Control 11 Data Recovery.
- CIS Control 12 Network Infrastructure Management.
- CIS Control 15 Service Provider Management.
- CIS Control 17 Incident Response Management.

## Limitations

- EU focus means sector/geopolitical patterns are not globally representative.
- DDoS event volume can dominate counts while ransomware may dominate impact.
- Forward-looking AI statements are outlook judgments rather than incident prevalence.
